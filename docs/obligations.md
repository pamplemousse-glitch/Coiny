# Obligations

External obligations binding Coiny (Athanor Works LLC), verified 2026-08-12 against branch
`fix/critical-backend-bugs` (commit `d8adad1`). Code claims cite file:line. External claims link
the authoritative source: the regulator, the statute, or the provider's own terms. Where a claim
could not be verified against a primary source it is marked "Unverified" with what would settle it.

Scope note: this document states which obligations exist, who imposes them, what compliance
requires, and how the code measures up. Where an obligation implies a measurable engineering
target (timeouts, staleness display, availability, user-count alarms), the number and its
verification method belong to the engineering budgets document (agent C). Where an obligation
implies a requirement that needs eliciting and structuring (consent flows, disclosure copy), the
method belongs to the spec methodology document (agent A). Each handoff is named inline.

Severity scale, shared across all four documents: BLOCKER (cannot launch with this open),
MAJOR (bites within the first 30 testers), MINOR (real but survivable), LATER (correct at scale,
premature now, trigger stated).

## 5. Threat model

Built from the actual architecture: opaque bearer sessions, three route scopes
(`backend/src/server.ts:96-152`), the Plaid webhook as the only push path, ~30 integrations in
three shapes. The two fixed cross-user exposures (D1 session mint, D2 shared Coinbase key) define
the most dangerous class: one user's financial picture shown to another. The BOLA/IDOR sweep
across all 45 tables came back clean (every `:id` route scopes by `userId`), so residual
cross-user risk lives in operator-level keys and config drift, not query predicates.

## 6. Data lifecycle: collection, retention, deletion, portability

**Collection.** Identity: Apple/Google `sub`, email encrypted at write
(`backend/src/store/users.ts:22`), display name. Financial: Plaid transactions with merchant
name, amount, date, category, plaintext at field level (`backend/src/db/schema.ts:99-108`);
cached balance scalars for ~20 integrations; per-user OAuth tokens and API keys encrypted
(section 4 rows); Spinwheel credit score as a plaintext integer (`schema.ts:165`). Sensitive
passthrough: phone number and date of birth are sent to Spinwheel for identity resolution but
not stored, verified against both Spinwheel tables (`schema.ts:159-166,371-377`). Device: APNs
tokens. Collection is close to the product minimum; the two avoidable items are full transaction
rows (if only aggregates drive the pet) and the plaintext credit score, which should be encrypted
under hard constraint 7 the next time that code is touched.

**Retention.** None exists: no purge job, no policy, no scheduler at all (D3). Three separate
authorities now demand one: Safeguards 314.4(c)(6) requires disposal within two years of last
use; Plaid's policy forbids storing data "other than as required to access or use the Services";
Discogs forbids caching "longer than is necessary" and displaying data older than six hours.
Write the policy sentence now ("transaction data is retained while the linked item is active plus
N days"), build the job when a scheduler exists. The Discogs six-hour rule cannot be satisfied at
all without one, which makes the scheduler a compliance dependency, not just a freshness feature.

**Deletion.** `DELETE /api/account` is honest about its mechanics: Plaid `itemRemove` per item,
failures logged and skipped so Plaid downtime cannot block the right to delete, then one
`deleteUser` cascading across 43 FK constraints (`backend/src/api/account.ts:23-40`). iOS now
signs out only on server-confirmed success and surfaces failure
(`ios/Coiny/Views/SettingsView.swift:85-97`). Remaining gaps, in priority order: non-Plaid OAuth
grants and the SnapTrade user are not revoked or deleted upstream (contractual for SnapTrade,
section 4); backups are unaddressed in user-facing text because no user-facing text exists.

**Portability.** Nothing. No export endpoint. No law requires it today. At UK launch it becomes
a UK GDPR right with a one-month clock; a JSON dump endpoint of the user's own rows makes DSARs a
non-event. Build it with the UK work, not before.

## 8. Open questions needing a lawyer, phrased as the questions to ask

1. "We aggregate consumers' financial account data via Plaid and display it back to them, free
   during TestFlight, later $99/yr. Are we 'significantly engaged in financial activities' and
   therefore a financial institution under the FTC Safeguards Rule from the first real bank
   connection, and does the answer change between free beta and paid launch?"
2. "At UK launch we will show UK users their own bank data sourced from TrueLayer, an authorised
   AISP whose end-user terms have the user contract with TrueLayer directly for the AIS. Do we
   need our own RAISP registration, can we operate as TrueLayer's agent, or does the perimeter
   guidance (PERG) put us outside regulated AIS because TrueLayer provides the service and we
   only consume its output? What does each option cost and how long does it take?"
3. "Draft one combined privacy notice satisfying the Reg P initial notice (12 CFR 1016.4),
   Plaid's Developer Policy disclosure requirement, and Apple 5.1.1. We share data only with
   service providers: does the annual-notice exception apply to us?"
4. "The household tier lets two adults see each other's linked accounts under one subscription.
   What consent must each grant, how do we handle one party revoking, and does GLBA or state law
   treat the second adult as our customer?"
5. "Our terms of service: liability cap, arbitration and class waiver, and an accuracy disclaimer
   for the displayed net worth. Is the disclaimer effective when we know specific failure modes
   make the number silently wrong, or must the product surface staleness for it to hold up?"
   (The product fix exists as defects D5/D6 regardless of the answer.)
6. "Does CFPB 1033 in its current posture, final rule effective January 2025 but under an ANPRM
   reconsideration since August 2025, impose any obligation on us directly as an authorized third
   party, or do our duties flow entirely through Plaid's contracts until the rulemaking settles?"
7. "Deleted users' data persists in database backups for the host's retention window, and today
   we revoke only Plaid upstream on deletion while other providers' OAuth grants survive. What
   must the privacy policy say, and is the current revocation posture defensible?"
8. "We display a user's credit score sourced from Spinwheel, which positions itself as a
   CRA-compliant consumer credit data platform. Does FCRA treat us as a user of consumer reports
   with a permissible-purpose obligation, or does consumer-directed delivery through Spinwheel's
   consent flow keep FCRA duties on their side? What must our Spinwheel contract say about this?"
9. "Kraken's global terms prohibit developing 'third-party applications that interact with Our
   Content' without prior written consent. Does a read-only balance display using the customer's
   own API key need Kraken's consent, and should we seek it in writing or drop to an
   integration-partner agreement?"
