# Coiny pre-launch verification: Part 5, Compliance (fintech-specific)

**Written 2026-08-15** against the working tree on branch
`docs/prelaunch-verification`. Every VERIFIED and FAILS row rests on a line of
this repository read on that date, or on a primary source fetched on that date
with the fetch recorded. UNVERIFIED rows name the instrument and the event that
would settle them.

Severity uses the PRD scale: **BLOCKER** (ships broken, breaks the law, or fails
review), **MAJOR** (materially wrong inside 30 testers), **MINOR** (real but
survivable), **LATER** (correct at scale, premature now, trigger stated).

**Scope boundary.** GLBA's Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314) is Part 1, sections 1.11
and 1.12, and is cross-referenced here rather than restated. This part covers the
finance-specific obligations a general checklist misses: the GLBA **Privacy**
Rule, state privacy law's GLBA carve-out, Plaid's contract and launch checklist,
Apple's and Google's finance-specific rules, subscription and auto-renewal law,
dark patterns, claims, disposal, open banking, and FCRA. It builds on
`docs/launch-gap-analysis.md` §3 and `docs/obligations.md` §1 and §2 rather than
re-deriving them.

**Source note.** `www.ftc.gov` returned HTTP 403 to every automated fetch on
2026-08-15, as it did for Part 1. Where an FTC source is cited, the row says
whether the text was read at a primary mirror (Cornell LII for the CFR, the
court's own PDF for the Eighth Circuit opinion) or is carried as secondary.
`www.ecfr.gov` was not used; Cornell LII mirrors the same regulatory text and
serves automated clients, per the brief's instruction.

## 5.2 The GLBA Privacy Rule (Regulation P), which is the obligation most likely to be missed

This is the subsection with the largest gap between what the repository believes
and what the rule requires, and the gap is not about content. Coiny's draft
privacy policy is a good Reg P notice. It is not a *delivered* notice, and Reg P
is a delivery rule as much as a content rule.

| # | What must be true | How it is verified | Status today | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.2.1 | Coiny owes an initial privacy notice, and the deadline is named | Read 12 CFR 1016.4(a)(1) | VERIFIED as an obligation, not discharged | The notice is owed to "An individual who becomes your customer, not later than when you establish a customer relationship" ([12 CFR 1016.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1016.4), fetched 2026-08-15). For Coiny the customer relationship begins at sign-in plus first connection, so the deadline is inside onboarding, not "before paid launch" |
| 5.2.2 | The notice content required by 1016.6(a) is present in the draft policy | Walk (a)(1) to (a)(9) against the draft | VERIFIED for seven of nine | (a)(1) categories collected: `docs/legal/privacy-policy.md:32-90`. (a)(2)/(a)(3) categories and recipients disclosed: `:104-147`. (a)(5) the 1016.13 service-provider statement: `:106-107`. (a)(6) opt-out: `:223-227` states correctly that there is nothing to opt out of. (a)(8) confidentiality and security practices: `:194-205`. (a)(7) FCRA affiliate sharing is inapplicable, there are no affiliates |
| 5.2.3 | The 1016.6(a)(4) former-customer statement is present | Search the draft for a statement about former customers | FAILS | The draft says what is deleted and when (`docs/legal/privacy-policy.md:149-173`) but never states its policy for disclosing nonpublic personal information about **former** customers, which is a distinct required item under 1016.6(a)(4). One sentence ("we do not disclose information about former customers except as described above") closes it. MINOR, and cheapest to fix in the attorney pass |
| 5.2.4 | Hosting the policy at a URL and linking it from Settings would discharge 1016.4 | Read the delivery rule, 12 CFR 1016.9 | FAILS, and this is the finding of this subsection | Delivery must be "so that each consumer can reasonably be expected to receive actual notice in writing or, if the consumer agrees, electronically" (1016.9(a)). For an online relationship the worked example is to "Post the notice on the electronic site and require the consumer to acknowledge receipt of the notice as a necessary step to obtaining a particular financial product or service" (1016.9(b)(1)(iii), [12 CFR 1016.9](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1016.9), fetched 2026-08-15). A link buried in Settings is neither posting at the point of the transaction nor an acknowledgment. MAJOR at the first real customer |
| 5.2.5 | The acknowledgment step exists somewhere in the product | Read the only screen every user passes | FAILS | `ios/Coiny/Views/SignInView.swift` has no privacy line, no Terms link and no policy link: `grep -rn "privacy\|Terms" ios/Coiny --include="*.swift"` returns only two `os.Logger` privacy attributes, in `Services/Telemetry.swift:109` and `Services/API+Telemetry.swift:43`. `ios/Coiny/Views/SettingsView.swift` (251 lines) contains no privacy, terms or contact link either. BLOCKER at the first real bank connection |
| 5.2.6 | The planned delivery vehicle is the right one | Read `docs/legal/consent-copy.md` against 1016.9(b)(1)(iii) | VERIFIED as a design, FAILS as a state | `docs/legal/consent-copy.md:18-28` specifies the exact line ("By continuing you agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy...") beneath the Sign in with Apple button, with both terms as tappable links, and says signing in constitutes consent. That is the 1016.9(b)(1)(iii) shape: the acknowledgment is a necessary step to obtaining the service. None of it is in the code. Half a day of iOS work once the URLs exist |
| 5.2.7 | The annual-notice exception applies, so Coiny owes one notice and not one per year | Read 12 CFR 1016.5(e) and check both conditions | VERIFIED, conditionally | The exception requires both that the institution shares nonpublic personal information with nonaffiliated third parties "only in accordance with" 1016.13, .14 or .15, and that it has "not changed your policies and practices with regard to disclosing nonpublic personal information" since the last notice ([12 CFR 1016.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1016.5), fetched 2026-08-15). Coiny shares only with service providers under contract (`docs/legal/service-providers.md`), so both hold today. This answers lawyer question Q3 in `docs/obligations.md` §8 on the annual half; the initial half is 5.2.4 |
| 5.2.8 | The annual-notice exception is understood as revocable by a product decision, not permanent | Name what breaks it | VERIFIED as a standing constraint | Adding any nonaffiliated recipient outside 1016.13/.14/.15, or changing the stated sharing practice, restarts the annual-notice duty and requires a revised notice. The concrete candidate in the repository is the unreconciled Sentry decision in `docs/launch-gap-analysis.md` §1 item 9: a third-party error processor that is not on the service-provider list. If Sentry is adopted, it must land with a policy sentence in the same PR, or the exception lapses silently |
| 5.2.9 | The choice not to use the model privacy form is made knowingly | Check whether a safe harbour is being declined | VERIFIED as a decision | Reg P carries a model privacy form in its appendix, referenced at 1016.6(f), and using it is the cheapest way to be certain the content is sufficient. Coiny's draft is plain-English prose, which is better product and worse safe harbour. Recommendation: keep the prose policy as the user-facing document, and have the attorney confirm content sufficiency in the review that founder checklist item 3 already schedules, rather than bolting the model form onto onboarding. Cost: zero extra, it is one line in an existing brief |

**Why this is the subsection to act on first.** Every other Reg P row is content,
and the content is already written and good. The delivery gap is the one that
makes an otherwise compliant document non-compliant, and it is the same half-day
of iOS work that closes Apple 3.1.2's Terms-of-Use link (5.5.1), Apple 5.1.1(i)'s
in-app policy link, Plaid's Link-setup consent item (5.4.2), and the telemetry
consent line (5.7.7). Four obligations, one screen.

## 5.6 Subscription and auto-renewal law, which is not the rule most people think it is

The single most common error in a 2026 compliance document is treating the FTC's
"click to cancel" Negative Option Rule as live. It is not. What binds the paywall
is ROSCA, which was never touched, and state automatic-renewal law, which is
stricter than ROSCA on disclosure wording and reaches any California subscriber.
